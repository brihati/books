# Pre-Conversation Interest Skill

Use this before approaching someone when you want to know whether a conversation with them is likely to be interesting.

The goal is not to decide whether the person is objectively interesting. The goal is to decide whether there is a promising path into a real conversation.

Most people are not interesting in the abstract. They become interesting when you find the right doorway: what they care about, what they have experienced, what they notice, what they have struggled with, what they are building, or what they are unusually opinionated about.

## Core Reframe

Do not ask:

> Is this person interesting?

Ask:

> What might be interesting about this person if I got curious enough?

Also ask:

> What topic might make them come alive?

> Is there enough signal here to start a respectful conversation?

This shift matters. If you judge too early, you look for reasons to dismiss them. If you get curious, you look for doors.

## Pre-Conversation Checklist

### 1. What visible clues do I have?

Look for external signals, but do not overinterpret them.

Ask yourself:

- What are they doing right now?
- What are they paying attention to?
- What are they wearing or carrying?
- Who are they with?
- What environment are they in?
- Do they seem relaxed, focused, rushed, bored, curious, or guarded?
- Is there anything distinctive about their style, tools, book, laptop stickers, conversation, behavior, or energy?

Examples:

- Someone reading a technical book may be into learning, career growth, or a niche field.
- Someone taking photos may care about aesthetics, memory, travel, or observation.
- Someone quietly watching the room may be thoughtful, shy, tired, or simply waiting.
- Someone with a specific fashion choice may care about identity, taste, culture, or self-expression.

Use clues only as possible entry points. Do not conclude too much from them.

### 2. What might they know that I do not?

Everyone has access to a world you do not have access to: their job, family, city, culture, failures, skills, friendships, private interests, unusual experiences, and opinions.

Ask yourself:

- What world might this person have lived in that I have not?
- What skill, job, hobby, culture, or life stage might they understand better than me?
- What could I learn from them in five minutes?

Instead of thinking:

> They look boring.

Think:

> There is probably some domain where they know more than I do. Can I find it?

### 3. What might they care about?

Interesting conversations usually happen when people talk about what they care about, not when they recite facts.

Ask yourself:

- What might this person be emotionally invested in?
- What topic would make their voice change?
- What might they defend, explain, complain about, or light up around?
- What are they choosing to spend time on?

The fastest path to an interesting conversation is often:

> choice -> reason -> emotion -> story

Useful follow-ups:

- What made you choose that?
- Why that instead of something else?
- What do you like about it?
- Was there a moment when you got really into it?

### 4. What part of them might be more complex than it looks?

This question prevents shallow judgment.

Ask yourself:

- What am I assuming about them based on appearance?
- Could the opposite be true?
- What might be going on beneath the surface?
- What would I notice if I gave them more credit?

Many people seem ordinary because they are not performing. Some people become interesting only after they feel safe, respected, or genuinely asked.

This is especially true for quieter people.

### 5. Am I expecting them to entertain me?

If your standard is "Does this person instantly impress me?" you will miss many good people.

Ask yourself:

- Am I looking for curiosity, or am I looking for a performance?
- Am I willing to contribute energy to the conversation?
- Am I judging them before giving them a chance?
- Am I expecting them to prove their value to me?

A better standard is:

> Can we create an interesting interaction together?

Conversation is co-created. Sometimes the person is not boring; the question is boring.

### 6. What would make this conversation enjoyable for them?

Before approaching, ask:

- Why would they want to talk to me?
- Am I interrupting them?
- Can I make the opening easy to answer?
- Can I give them a graceful way out?
- Can I start with something light instead of demanding depth immediately?

People open up when they feel respected, not inspected.

A good conversation does not feel like an interview, interrogation, or test.

### 7. What is my honest reason for wanting to talk to them?

Ask yourself:

- Am I curious?
- Attracted?
- Networking?
- Lonely?
- Trying to be social?
- Looking for help?
- Looking for status?
- Trying to avoid someone else?

Your motive affects your energy.

If you are honest with yourself, you can approach more cleanly:

- If you are curious, ask something genuine.
- If you are attracted, do not hide behind fake business questions forever.
- If you are networking, respect their time.
- If you are lonely, do not put pressure on them to fill the gap.

### 8. Is this a good moment?

Even if someone seems interesting, the timing may be wrong.

Ask yourself:

- Are they busy?
- Are they working?
- Are they wearing headphones?
- Are they in the middle of an intense conversation?
- Are they physically moving somewhere?
- Do they seem open to interruption?
- Is the setting socially appropriate?

Good moments:

- Waiting in line
- Before or after an event
- During a casual gathering
- Shared table or social setting
- After a talk, class, or session
- When they make eye contact or seem open

Bad moments:

- They are typing intensely
- They are clearly rushing
- They are in a private conversation
- They are reading with full focus
- They give closed body language
- They answer minimally and do not reciprocate

A person can be interesting and still not available.

### 9. What is one low-pressure opening I can use?

Before approaching, decide on one simple opening.

Good openings are:

- specific
- easy to answer
- not too personal
- not overly clever
- connected to the situation

Examples:

- How did you find this event?
- Have you been here before?
- That book caught my eye. How are you finding it?
- I heard you mention product design. What kind of work do you do?
- You seemed to have a strong reaction to that point. Did you agree with it?
- I am trying to meet a few people here. What brought you?

Avoid openings that are too heavy too soon:

- What is your life purpose?
- What is your deepest trauma?
- Are you an interesting person?
- What do you bring to the table?

### 10. What is my exit plan?

A good conversationalist knows how to leave cleanly.

Before approaching, ask:

- If they are not interested, how will I exit gracefully?
- If the conversation is flat, how long will I give it?
- Can I leave without making it awkward?

Simple exits:

- Nice talking to you. I am going to say hi to a few others.
- I will let you get back to it.
- Good meeting you. Hope you enjoy the rest of the event.
- I am going to grab a drink, but it was nice chatting.

Having an exit plan makes you less needy and more relaxed.

## Quick Scoring System

Before talking to someone, score five signals from 0 to 2.

### 1. Context Signal

Is there a natural reason to talk?

- 0: No shared context
- 1: Same place, event, or situation
- 2: Clear shared context or mutual activity

### 2. Curiosity Signal

Do you have something you genuinely want to know?

- 0: Not really
- 1: Mild curiosity
- 2: Clear curiosity

### 3. Openness Signal

Do they seem approachable?

- 0: Closed, busy, unavailable
- 1: Neutral
- 2: Relaxed, socially open, making eye contact

### 4. Distinctiveness Signal

Is there anything specific to ask about?

- 0: Nothing noticeable
- 1: Some possible clue
- 2: Strong clue, such as a book, work, comment, style, behavior, or shared topic

### 5. Personal Energy Signal

Do you have enough energy to be interested and present?

- 0: No
- 1: Maybe
- 2: Yes

Interpretation:

- 0-3: Probably skip unless necessary.
- 4-6: Try a light, low-pressure opener.
- 7-10: Worth approaching.

This is not a moral judgment of the person. It is a practical read of whether this is a good conversation opportunity.

## Compact Self-Questions

Before talking to someone, ask:

1. What might this person know that I do not?
2. What might they care about?
3. What clue tells me where to begin?
4. What assumption am I making about them?
5. What could make them more interesting than they first appear?
6. What would be easy and pleasant for them to answer?
7. Am I genuinely curious, or just looking to be entertained?
8. Is this a good time to approach?
9. What is one natural opening question?
10. How can I exit gracefully if there is no connection?

## Questions That Reveal Whether Someone Is Interesting

The best questions usually invite:

- stories
- choices
- opinions
- tradeoffs
- surprises
- lessons
- taste
- change over time

### Easy Openers

Use these when you do not know the person yet:

- What brought you here?
- How do you know people here?
- Have you been to one of these before?
- What is your connection to this?
- How has your day been so far?
- What have you been working on lately?

### Interest-Finding Questions

These help you discover what they care about:

- What has been taking up most of your attention lately?
- What are you most into these days?
- What do you do when you have a completely free day?
- What is something you have been learning recently?
- What is something you are unusually picky about?
- What is a topic you can talk about for hours?

### Story Questions

These are stronger than factual questions:

- How did you get into that?
- What was the moment you realized you liked it?
- What is the weirdest part of that world?
- What is a mistake people make when they first get into it?
- Was there a turning point for you?
- What is the most memorable thing that happened through that?

### Depth Questions

Use these only after some rapport:

- What changed your mind about that?
- What do you understand now that you did not five years ago?
- What has been harder than you expected?
- What are you trying to get better at?
- What do people misunderstand about you?
- What is something you care about more than most people around you?

### Taste Questions

Taste reveals personality quickly:

- What is something you think is underrated?
- What is something popular that you do not really get?
- What makes something good in your opinion?
- Whose work do you respect?
- What is your standard for quality in that?

### Energy Questions

These reveal aliveness:

- What has been surprisingly fun lately?
- What are you looking forward to?
- What gives you energy?
- What kind of people do you enjoy being around?
- What conversations do you never get tired of?

## Questions To Avoid Early

Avoid questions that feel like a test:

- What do you do? if said mechanically as the first question
- Where do you see yourself in five years?
- What is your biggest weakness?
- What is your purpose?
- Why are you single?
- How much money do you make?
- Are you successful?
- What value do you provide?

Also avoid trying to be impressive too early. If you are performing, you are not listening.

## How To Tell If They Are Becoming Interesting

Good signs:

- Their face becomes more animated
- They give longer answers
- They add details without being pushed
- They ask you questions back
- They tell a story
- They reveal preferences or opinions
- They laugh or become more relaxed
- They say "Actually..." or "It is funny because..."
- They correct or refine your understanding
- They mention something specific, not generic

Weak signs:

- They give one-word answers
- They do not ask anything back
- They keep scanning the room
- They turn their body away
- They answer politely but flatly
- They do not expand on any topic
- You feel like you are carrying the whole thing

If you get weak signs, do not force depth. Try one different topic, then exit gracefully.

## The Three Door Method

If you are trying to find what makes someone interesting, use three doors.

### Door 1: Context

Ask about the shared situation:

- What brought you here?
- How are you finding this?
- Do you know many people here?

### Door 2: Current Attention

Ask what is alive in their life now:

- What has been occupying you lately?
- What are you working on these days?
- What has been interesting to you recently?

### Door 3: Personal Taste

Ask what they like, dislike, notice, or value:

- What are you unusually picky about?
- What is something you think is underrated?
- What kind of work do you respect?

If none of the three doors opens, the conversation may not be worth continuing right now.

## Types Of Interesting

People are interesting in different ways:

- Story-interesting: they have lived unusual experiences.
- Mind-interesting: they think clearly or differently.
- Taste-interesting: they have sharp preferences.
- Skill-interesting: they are good at something.
- Emotion-interesting: they feel deeply and honestly.
- World-interesting: they come from a world you do not know.
- Question-interesting: they ask unusually good questions.
- Presence-interesting: they make you feel seen or calm.
- Builder-interesting: they are making something.
- Contradiction-interesting: they contain surprising combinations.

Before dismissing someone, ask:

> What type of interesting might this person be?

Not everyone is loud-interesting. Not everyone is funny-interesting. Not everyone is impressive-interesting. Some people are quietly fascinating.

## Internal Script Before Approaching

Use this before walking up to someone:

> I do not know if they are interesting yet. I only know I have a possible clue. My job is to approach respectfully, ask one easy question, listen for what they care about, and see if there is energy. If there is not, I can leave cleanly.

## Examples

### At An Event

You see someone standing alone at a conference.

Ask yourself:

- What is the shared context? The event.
- Are they open? They are standing alone, not on their phone.
- What might they care about? The topic of the event.
- What is an easy opener?

Say:

> Hey, I am trying to meet a few people here. What brought you to this event?

If they say:

> I work in growth, so I came for the panel.

Follow with:

> What part of growth are you most focused on right now?

Then:

> What has been harder than expected?

### At A Cafe

Someone is reading a book.

Ask yourself:

- Are they deeply focused? If yes, do not interrupt.
- Is there a natural opening? Maybe if they look up or seem relaxed.
- Am I curious about the book?

Say:

> Sorry to interrupt briefly. I noticed the book. Is it good?

If they answer warmly, continue:

> What made you pick it up?

If they answer shortly, exit:

> Nice. I will let you get back to it.

### At A Party

Someone seems quiet.

Ask yourself:

- Am I assuming they are boring because they are quiet?
- What low-pressure question could help them open?
- What would be easier than "What do you do?"

Say:

> How do you know the host?

Then:

> What kind of things do you usually enjoy outside of work?

If they mention something specific:

> How did you get into that?

Quiet people often open through specific, non-performative questions.

### Professional Networking

You meet someone in your field.

Avoid:

> So what do you do?

Better:

> What kind of problems are you working on these days?

Then:

> What is the interesting part of that?

> What is the frustrating part?

> What do people outside the field misunderstand about it?

This gets beyond job titles.

## Better Versions Of Common Questions

Do not ask only for their resume. Ask for their relationship to something.

Instead of:

> What do you do?

Ask:

> What are you working on that has your attention right now?

Instead of:

> Where are you from?

Ask:

> What was it like growing up there?

Instead of:

> What are your hobbies?

Ask:

> What is something you got into deeper than you expected?

Instead of:

> Do you like your job?

Ask:

> What part of your work do you find most satisfying or annoying?

## One-Sentence Version

Before talking to someone, look for a clue, form a generous hypothesis, ask an easy question, listen for energy, follow the energy, and leave gracefully if it is not there.

## Memorize This

Ask yourself:

> What might make this person come alive?

Then ask them a question that gives them room to show you.
