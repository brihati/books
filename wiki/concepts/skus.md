- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-05-step-2` / `chapter-05-step-2-chunk-006`
  - The chunk covers modular packaging for SaaS products and includes a case study of Medallia's use of marketecture to package hidden product differentiators as distinct modules. It also discusses decision-making criteria for whether to introduce features as add-ons versus incorporating them into main packages.
  - Use case names in packaging need not be final module names
  - Packaging hypothesis should be tested against customer segments to determine package-segment fit
  - Hidden capabilities in enterprise software can be creatively packaged as differentiators
- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-08-step-5a` / `chapter-08-step-5a-chunk-005`
  - Discussion of Pricing Calculators and CPQ (Configure-Price-Quote) systems as tools for scaling pricing operations. Covers best practices for calculator design, the definition and benefits of CPQ, and challenges in CPQ implementation.
  - Pricing calculators are simpler than CPQ but still require significant time investment to build correctly
  - CPQ imposes guardrails that force sales reps to adhere strictly to the pricing model
  - CPQ implementation typically takes 2-3x longer than initially estimated
- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-11-pricing-example` / `chapter-11-pricing-example-chunk-005`
  - This chunk presents the finalized pricing and packaging model for a customer support product with two tiers (Pro and Elite) based on WTP survey feedback. It details the pricing structure using a 3-part tariff with volume discounts and overage pricing, along with operational decisions for go-to-market implementation.
  - Mid-market customers prioritize support cost reduction and are more price sensitive; enterprise customers prioritize customer satisfaction and agility
  - Mid-market customers show highest WTP at 30 cents per interaction (85% willing); enterprise at 50 cents per interaction (55% willing)
  - 3-part tariff pricing model selected for highest revenue uplift potential
- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-16-interviews-case-studies-with-top-pricing-leaders` / `chapter-16-interviews-case-studies-with-top-pricing-leaders-chunk-011`
  - Discussion of the importance of revenue recognition in pricing strategy and the selection of appropriate technology platforms, particularly Zuora, for managing recurring revenue pricing models and subscription lifecycle management.
  - Revenue recognition (rev rec) is critical and must be coordinated across legal, contracts, billing, and quoting
  - Don't begin building a product without discussing pricing and usage tracking
  - Zuora provides a recurring revenue platform that handles CPQ, billing, usage data, payments, and tax
- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-16-interviews-case-studies-with-top-pricing-leaders` / `chapter-16-interviews-case-studies-with-top-pricing-leaders-chunk-016`
  - Interview with Mehul Sahni of Rubrik about the company's transition from a perpetual licensing model to a subscription-based pricing model in the backup and recovery industry. Includes discussion of CPQ systems, pricing strategy consulting for different company stages, and market disruption through simplified product offerings.
  - Annual review of existing product pricing should be more fruitful with better information
  - CPQ tools may be overkill; companies with 100 SKUs often benefit more from reducing to 12-15 structured packages
  - Large public companies typically have dedicated pricing teams, but early-stage companies gain highest leverage from external pricing consulting
