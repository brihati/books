- [[books/the-mom-test|The Mom Test]] / `chapter-04-avoiding-bad-data` / `chapter-04-avoiding-bad-data-chunk-002`
  - The chunk discusses how to recognize and avoid two major types of bad data in customer interviews: compliments (which are vacuous and worthless) and fluff (generic claims, future-tense promises, and hypothetical statements). It teaches techniques for anchoring fluff by asking for concrete past examples instead of generic or hypothetical responses.
  - Compliments are pervasive in conversations and trick founders into thinking conversations 'went well' when they actually contain no real data
  - Fluff comes in three forms: generic claims ('I usually', 'I always', 'I never'), future-tense promises ('I would', 'I will'), and hypothetical maybes ('I might', 'I could')
  - The deadliest fluff is 'I would definitely buy that' because it sounds concrete but represents wildly optimistic future behavior
