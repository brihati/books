- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-03-the-5-step-pricing-transformation-framework` / `chapter-03-the-5-step-pricing-transformation-framework-chunk-001`
  - Introduction to the 5-Step Pricing Transformation Framework, a sequential process for pricing strategy that covers Goals & Segments, Positioning & Packaging, Price Metric, Rate-setting, and Operationalization. The framework addresses how pricing is a strategic exercise touching all business areas, and discusses common pitfalls and considerations at each stage.
  - Pricing is a strategic exercise that touches every part of a business, not just placing a price point on a product
  - Most pricing problems stem from misalignment within the executive team at the Goals & Segments stage
  - Alignment is key: when everyone is on the same page about strategy and segments, everything else flows more smoothly
- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-07-step-4` / `chapter-07-step-4-chunk-001`
  - Step 4 covers rate setting by determining how much to charge for products. It explains five factors influencing pricing: Prospect Willingness To Pay (WTP), Competitive & Category Price Points, Cost of Delivery, Discounting For Procurement, and Pricing Strategy. The chapter focuses on WTP and presents three methods for finding it when lacking market feedback: surveys, conjoint analysis, and in-pers
  - Pricing is determined by five factors: WTP, competitive price points, cost of delivery, discounting, and pricing strategy
  - Two distinct scenarios require different WTP discovery tactics: lacking market feedback versus having some market feedback
  - Basic pricing survey uses two anchoring questions about price acceptability
- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-07-step-4` / `chapter-07-step-4-chunk-002`
  - Explanation of Van Westendorp analysis and conjoint analysis as pricing research techniques. Van Westendorp identifies price ranges through intersection points of customer perception curves. Conjoint analysis determines how customers value different product attributes and features through survey-based choice experiments.
  - Van Westendorp analysis identifies Price of Marginal Cheapness (PMC) and Price of Marginal Expensiveness (PME) as acceptable price bounds
  - Van Westendorp method is better suited for easily explainable products like CPG where price and quality are strongly linked
  - Van Westendorp provides directional guidance that should be combined with hypothesis, qualitative interviews, and strategic intent

- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-16-interviews-case-studies-with-top-pricing-leaders` / `chapter-16-interviews-case-studies-with-top-pricing-leaders-chunk-007`
  - Pranav Kashyap from Mixpanel describes the company's transition from an events-based pricing model (since 2009) to a monthly tracked users (MTUs) model in 2019, including customer segmentation, repackaging of offerings, and the criteria used to evaluate alternative pricing metrics.
  - Pricing implementation requires partnership with sales ops, IT, and finance
  - Events-based pricing didn't work because customers couldn't predict event volume and didn't see business value correlated with event tracking
  - Mixpanel identified three main customer segments: Engineering/Product/Design teams, Data teams, and Marketing teams, each with different use cases
- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-16-interviews-case-studies-with-top-pricing-leaders` / `chapter-16-interviews-case-studies-with-top-pricing-leaders-chunk-014`
  - Joshua Bloom of Simon-Kucher & Partners discusses the A-Z of pricing projects for SaaS companies, including the three-phase engagement process (diagnostic, primary research, implementation), pricing challenges across different company stages, and how to select appropriate pricing structures based on predictability, usage growth rates, and correlation to compute/data costs.
  - Billing systems must provide flexibility to handle co-terming issues and varied purchase patterns without confusing customers
  - Finance and collection capabilities are critical; high sales volume means nothing if you cannot collect from customers
  - The diagnostic phase establishes a straw-man view before customer conversations, using internal data and pattern matching from competitors
