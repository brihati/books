## Source Evidence

- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-06-step-3` / `chapter-06-step-3-chunk-002`
  - Analysis of pricing metric changes at three established SaaS companies (Kustomer, Mixpanel, HubSpot) during their growth stages, showing how they adapted pricing models to better align with customer value perception and simplify sales processes.
  - Second pricing metric achieved 10x increase in revenue capture
  - Customers prefer repeatable, consistent pricing that maps to existing budgets over innovative but unfamiliar models
  - Changing pricing metrics can reduce customer churn when bills no longer feel disproportionate to value
- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-07-step-4` / `chapter-07-step-4-chunk-004`
  - Detailed framework for conducting customer discovery interviews to validate pricing, packaging, and product positioning, followed by post-interview debrief processes and data analysis methodologies for assessing product-market fit and pricing strategy.
  - Force-rank pre-created pain point statements to validate hypotheses across customers
  - Conduct 15-20 minute product demo after pain point assessment
  - Use $100 distribution exercise for both pain points and packaging benefits analysis
- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-07-step-4` / `chapter-07-step-4-chunk-005`
  - Using existing customer pricing data to understand market pricing, identify discounting patterns, calculate unit pricing across customer segments, and validate packaging approach by analyzing feature usage correlation with customer value.
  - Create rank-ordered list of customers by pricing to visualize pricing distribution
  - Large customers tend to pay 2-10x more than Small customers
  - List prices are 30% to 50% higher than actual per account ARR for Large customers
- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-16-interviews-case-studies-with-top-pricing-leaders` / `chapter-16-interviews-case-studies-with-top-pricing-leaders-chunk-018`
  - Discussion of value metrics selection for SaaS products, market benchmarking approaches, and differences between commercial and enterprise pricing models. Uses Office 365 backup products and disaster recovery solutions as examples of metric standardization and pricing strategy.
  - Selecting the right value metric is more strategic than simply determining price and willingness to pay
  - Market consensus and customer familiarity with a metric should guide pricing decisions
  - Pricing on a metric different from market standards can be disruptive or counterproductive
