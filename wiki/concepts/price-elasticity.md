- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-05-step-2` / `chapter-05-step-2-chunk-004`
  - The chunk discusses Amplitude's pricing strategy and how to create effective price packaging. It covers the evolution of pricing strategies, principles for packaging products into tiers (gradation, package-segment fit, no shelf-ware), and a framework for deciding on price transparency based on market size and homogeneity.
  - Amplitude does not expose pricing for Growth and Enterprise plans to capture greater value from clients with higher price elasticity
  - Free tiers make sense only if going after large TAM or wanting to undercut competitors
  - Amplitude's pricing evolved from 2016 to 2020 as they became a more trusted brand with more features
- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-05-step-2` / `chapter-05-step-2-chunk-005`
  - Discussion of modular packaging as a flexible pricing approach that allows tailored offerings to different customer segments. The approach involves mapping product features to use cases, ensuring features are MECE, and estimating value perception across customer segments to determine which modules should be bundled in base packages versus offered as add-ons.
  - Modular packaging enables capturing greater revenue than standardized packages
  - Prevents both shelfware (unused capabilities) and missing value delivery
  - Features must be MECE (Mutually Exclusive Completely Exhaustive) to avoid duplication across modules
- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-07-step-4` / `chapter-07-step-4-chunk-004`
  - Detailed framework for conducting customer discovery interviews to validate pricing, packaging, and product positioning, followed by post-interview debrief processes and data analysis methodologies for assessing product-market fit and pricing strategy.
  - Force-rank pre-created pain point statements to validate hypotheses across customers
  - Conduct 15-20 minute product demo after pain point assessment
  - Use $100 distribution exercise for both pain points and packaging benefits analysis
- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-07-step-4` / `chapter-07-step-4-chunk-007`
  - The chunk discusses why ROI-based pricing is problematic, explores three market types (Blue Ocean, Monopolistic, Renaissance) and their pricing implications, explains why cost-plus pricing doesn't work well for software, and distinguishes between average cost and marginal cost in SaaS pricing decisions.
  - Most successful software companies do not sell based on ROI
  - ROI calculations are difficult to defend and often unrealistic when measured at year-end
  - ROI is better used for price justification than for actual pricing decisions
