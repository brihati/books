## Source Evidence

- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-04-step-1` / `chapter-04-step-1-chunk-001`
  - This chapter introduces segmentation as the first step in pricing strategy, explaining how to divide customer bases into focused groups. It contrasts two common B2B SaaS segments (mid-market and enterprise) and introduces the concept of Ideal Customer Profiles (ICPs) with specific attributes. It warns against ICP Drift and its negative impact on pricing strategy alignment.
  - Segmentation divides potential customer base into smaller groups with shared characteristics and needs
  - Mid-market companies are price-sensitive, value simplicity and speed, need easy implementation without heavy resource requirements
  - Enterprise customers prioritize security, compliance, customization, and support; willing to invest significant time and money
- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-07-step-4` / `chapter-07-step-4-chunk-004`
  - Detailed framework for conducting customer discovery interviews to validate pricing, packaging, and product positioning, followed by post-interview debrief processes and data analysis methodologies for assessing product-market fit and pricing strategy.
  - Force-rank pre-created pain point statements to validate hypotheses across customers
  - Conduct 15-20 minute product demo after pain point assessment
  - Use $100 distribution exercise for both pain points and packaging benefits analysis
- [[books/price-to-scale|Price to Scale]] / `chapter-07-step-4` / `chapter-07-step-4-chunk-007`
  - The chunk discusses why ROI-based pricing is problematic, explores three market types (Blue Ocean, Monopolistic, Renaissance) and their pricing implications, explains why cost-plus pricing doesn't work well for software, and distinguishes between average cost and marginal cost in SaaS pricing decisions.
  - Most successful software companies do not sell based on ROI
  - ROI calculations are difficult to defend and often unrealistic when measured at year-end
  - ROI is better used for price justification than for actual pricing decisions
