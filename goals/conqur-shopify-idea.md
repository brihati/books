# Conqur Shopify Idea

We are currently ideating on several related Shopify-focused product directions. These ideas overlap with each other, and we are still trying to identify the ideal audience through user conversations.

## 1. Shopify AI Developer

We have already built a Shopify AI developer.

Users can describe what they want to build in plain English, and the platform can generate Shopify sections or complete pages for them.

## 2. AI Product Storytelling For Brands

We are focusing on brands that care deeply about craft, storytelling, and connection, especially brands that want their product pages, campaign pages, and landing pages to communicate a clear brand point of view.

Every brand has a story: a point of view it wants people to walk away with. Today, that story is usually told the exact same way to every visitor on a product page, regardless of who they are, what they care about, or what brought them there.

The opportunity is to build an AI personalization layer that adapts how a product story is told to each visitor on PDPs, campaign pages, or landing pages while staying true to the brand's aesthetic, values, voice, and positioning.

Examples:

- A floral dress means something different to someone planning a beach escape than someone getting ready for a night out.
- A sofa means something different to someone furnishing their first apartment on a budget than someone redoing a living room for a family with kids and pets.

This idea is currently in beta.

## 3. Intent-Based Storytelling And A/B Testing

This idea is tied to product pages, campaign pages, and landing pages themselves.

Brands would be able to adapt and A/B test product stories, page sections, and changes based on user intent. This could include testing different storytelling angles that still fit the brand narrative or campaign story.

This idea is currently in beta as part of the broader AI product storytelling direction.

## Current Focus

The last two ideas are still in the discovery stage. We are currently talking to users to understand which audience is the best fit and where the strongest demand exists.

The goal is not just to validate whether people like the ideas. The goal is to identify which Shopify customer has a painful, repeated, budgeted problem that Conqur can win first.

## Candidate Ideal Customers

### Shopify Agencies

Agencies are constantly asked to build sections, landing pages, campaign pages, theme changes, and CRO variants for merchants.

Possible pain:

- Client requests are frequent and often urgent.
- Small theme or landing-page changes consume senior developer time.
- Margins suffer when work is custom but budgets are fixed.
- Clients want fast iteration, but agency delivery still depends on design, development, QA, and deployment cycles.

Current workaround:

- Internal Shopify developers.
- Freelancers or overflow contractors.
- Reusable section libraries.
- Page builders.
- Manual Liquid development.

Buying signal to test:

- Do they have enough repeated Shopify build work to justify paying for automation?
- Would they trust AI-generated code in client projects?
- Would the tool increase margin, reduce turnaround time, or let them take on more client work?

### In-House Ecommerce Teams

These are Shopify brands with internal ecommerce, growth, or merchandising teams that need frequent site changes.

Possible pain:

- Marketing wants campaigns live faster than developers can support.
- Engineering queues block revenue-facing experiments.
- Teams need brand-consistent PDP stories, landing pages, and collection-page updates.
- Non-technical operators cannot safely edit Liquid or build custom sections.

Current workaround:

- Internal dev team.
- Agency retainers.
- Page builders such as Replo or Shogun.
- Figma handoff to developers.
- Theme templates and duplicated pages.

Buying signal to test:

- How often do they launch or update PDPs, landing pages, or campaign pages?
- How long does a typical page or theme change take today?
- How much revenue depends on speed of campaign execution?
- Who owns the budget: marketing, ecommerce, growth, or engineering?

### DTC Founders And Lean Shopify Teams

These are smaller brands where the founder, marketer, or operator handles most site updates.

Possible pain:

- They cannot afford frequent agency or developer help.
- They want professional-looking pages without learning Shopify development.
- They need to move quickly for ads, product launches, PDP updates, and promotions.
- They care about brand voice but do not have enough copy, design, or dev support to tailor product stories by audience.

Current workaround:

- Off-the-shelf themes.
- Shopify page builders.
- Freelancers.
- Canva/Figma plus manual Shopify editing.

Buying signal to test:

- Do they have enough recurring need, or is this mostly occasional?
- Are they willing to pay for speed and quality, or do they expect a cheap page-builder replacement?
- Is their brand standard high enough that generic AI output is not acceptable?

### CRO And Performance Marketing Teams

These teams care about PDP and landing-page velocity, paid traffic conversion, and experiment throughput.

Possible pain:

- Paid campaigns need message-matched landing pages.
- A/B tests are limited by development bandwidth.
- Different audience intents need different product-story and page angles.
- The team has ideas but cannot ship variants fast enough.

Current workaround:

- Dedicated landing-page tools.
- CRO agencies.
- Internal design and dev teams.
- Manual duplication of pages and sections.

Buying signal to test:

- How many tests, PDP story variants, or landing pages do they want to run per month?
- What blocks them from running more tests?
- Do they already have traffic volume and analytics maturity?
- Would intent-based product storytelling improve conversion enough to matter?

### Shopify Plus Brands

These are larger merchants with more budget, more operational complexity, and higher brand standards.

Possible pain:

- Site changes involve more stakeholders and slower approval cycles.
- Brand consistency matters across every PDP, page, and campaign.
- Teams may have enough budget for tooling if it reduces dev dependency or increases experiment velocity.

Current workaround:

- Internal ecommerce and engineering teams.
- Retained agencies.
- Enterprise CRO tools.
- Custom theme systems.

Buying signal to test:

- Are they open to AI-generated storefront code or pages?
- What governance, QA, and approval controls would they require?
- Is the pain urgent enough, or are existing agency/dev workflows acceptable?

## Core Problems To Validate

### 1. Shopify Build Bottleneck

The customer wants to create or modify Shopify storefront experiences faster than their current development process allows.

This maps most directly to the Shopify AI Developer.

Strong signal:

- They frequently request new sections, templates, page changes, or theme updates.
- Turnaround time is a recurring frustration.
- They already pay developers or agencies for this work.
- They can point to recent delayed launches or missed revenue opportunities.

Weak signal:

- They only need occasional changes.
- Their current page builder is good enough.
- They are curious about AI but do not have a specific operational bottleneck.

### 2. Brand-Consistent Product Story Production

The customer needs PDPs, campaign pages, and landing pages that match brand voice, design standards, product positioning, campaign context, and the story the brand wants visitors to take away.

This maps most directly to AI Product Storytelling For Brands.

Strong signal:

- They launch campaigns, products, advertorials, seasonal offers, PDP updates, or audience-specific pages often.
- They already spend money on page design, copywriting, development, or CRO support.
- Existing AI tools produce output that feels too generic or off-brand.
- They care deeply about craft, storytelling, and emotional connection, not just conversion mechanics.

Weak signal:

- They do not launch pages frequently.
- They mostly need basic page templates.
- Brand quality is not a high priority.

### 3. Intent-Based Storytelling Experimentation

The customer wants PDPs, landing pages, and storefront changes that adapt the product story to visitor intent, campaign source, audience segment, funnel stage, or the storytelling angle most relevant to that visitor.

This maps most directly to Intent-Based Storytelling And A/B Testing.

Strong signal:

- They already run paid acquisition or CRO experiments.
- They have enough traffic to measure variants.
- They can describe specific user intents they want to target.
- They can describe different contexts in which the same product means different things to different shoppers.
- They are blocked by variant creation speed, not by lack of test ideas.

Weak signal:

- They do not have enough traffic for meaningful testing.
- They are not already measuring conversion.
- They want AI personalization in theory but cannot name the decision it would improve.

## Discovery Questions

Use these questions to learn about past behavior, current workarounds, budget, and urgency. Avoid asking whether they would use the product.

### Workflow And Frequency

- Tell me about the last time you needed a new Shopify landing page, section, or storefront change.
- What triggered that request?
- Who was involved from idea to launch?
- How long did it take from request to production?
- How often does this happen in a typical month?
- How often do you change PDP copy, sections, or creative based on campaign, audience, or product context?

### Pain And Cost

- What was the hardest part of getting that page or change live?
- Where did it get delayed?
- What happened because of the delay?
- How much internal or external time went into it?
- What do you currently spend on developers, agencies, page builders, or CRO tools?

### Current Alternatives

- What tools or people do you use today for landing pages and Shopify changes?
- What do you like about that workflow?
- What still feels broken or slow?
- Have you tried AI tools for this already?
- What made the output usable or unusable?
- Where does your product storytelling live today: PDP copy, landing pages, ads, email, advertorials, social, or somewhere else?
- When the same product is shown to different audiences, what changes in the story you want to tell?

### Trust And Adoption

- Who would need to approve AI-generated Shopify code or pages?
- What would make you reject an automatically generated page?
- What QA, review, or brand controls would you need before publishing?
- Would this be used by marketers, developers, designers, or agencies?

### Commitment

- If we could help with this, who else on your team should we speak with?
- Do you have an upcoming campaign or page build where this problem will happen again?
- Would you be open to testing this on one real page or theme change?
- What would need to be true for this to replace part of your current workflow?

## ICP Decision Criteria

Use these criteria to compare customer segments after conversations.

| Criterion | What To Look For |
|---|---|
| Frequency | They need Shopify PDPs, pages, sections, variants, or changes every week or month. |
| Urgency | Delays affect campaign launches, revenue, conversion, or client satisfaction. |
| Budget | They already pay for developers, agencies, copywriting, CRO tools, or landing-page software. |
| Pain | They can describe recent examples, not just abstract interest. |
| Buyer Access | The person feeling the pain can influence or control budget. |
| Trust Path | There is a believable path for them to trust AI-generated output. |
| Differentiation | Existing page builders or AI tools do not already solve the core pain. |
| Referenceability | Customers in the segment know each other or can become credible proof points. |

## Current Hypothesis

The strongest early audience is likely one of these:

1. Shopify agencies that need to increase delivery speed and margin on repeated storefront work.
2. In-house ecommerce or growth teams at brands that frequently update PDPs, launch campaigns, and care about product storytelling.
3. CRO or performance marketing teams that already have traffic, test ideas, and a bottleneck around page or variant production.

DTC founders and very small teams may be easier to reach, but they may have weaker budgets and less frequent pain. Shopify Plus brands may have stronger budgets, but they may require more trust, governance, and integration before adopting.

## Next Learning Goal

The next user conversations should determine which segment has the strongest combination of frequency, urgency, budget, and trust.

The most important thing to learn is not whether people like AI-generated Shopify pages. The most important thing is whether they have a recurring workflow where slow PDP storytelling, page production, or storefront experimentation already costs them time, money, clients, or revenue.
