Generative Analogies Some analogies are so useful that they don't merely shed light on a concept, they actually become platforms for novel thinking. For ex- ample, the metaphor of the brain as a computer has been central to the insights generated by cognitive psychologists during the past fifty years. It's easier to define how a computer works than to define how the brain works. For this reason it can be fruitful for psychologists to

use various, well-understood aspects of a computer—such as mem- ory, buffers, or processors —as inspiration to locate similar functions in the brain. Good metaphors are "generative." The psychologist Donald Schon introduced this term to describe metaphors that generate "new per- ceptions, explanations, and inventions."
