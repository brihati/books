# 12. When Nothing Else Works, Try This

## Detailed Chapter Summary

This chapter teaches Carnegie's principle: **Throw down a challenge.** A well-framed challenge can awaken pride, competition, and the desire to excel when other appeals fail. The material frames influence as an exercise in human dignity: people become more receptive when they feel understood, respected, important, and free from humiliation. The chapter's examples and anecdotes are used to turn that principle into behavior a reader can practice in ordinary conversations, sales situations, leadership moments, criticism, disagreement, and requests for cooperation.

## Key Points

- Core principle: Throw down a challenge.
- The practical aim is to reduce defensiveness and increase willing cooperation.
- Carnegie emphasizes the other person's pride, desires, interests, and point of view as the route to influence.
- The method depends on sincerity; mechanical flattery or manipulative technique undercuts the lesson.
- The chapter is best used as a behavioral rule: apply it before trying to persuade, correct, sell, lead, or ask.

## Important Examples and Stories

- 12 When Nothing Else Works, Try This Charles Schwab had a mill manager whose people weren’t producing their quota of work.
- “How is it,” Schwab asked him, “that a manager as capable as you can’t make this mill turn out what it should?” “I don’t know,” the manager replied.
- Schwab asked the manager for a piece of chalk, then, turning to the nearest man, asked: “How many heats did your shift make today?” “Six.” Without another word, Schwab chalked a big figure six on the floor, and walked away.
- He chalked it down on the floor.” The next morning Schwab walked through the mill again.

## Definitions and Terms

- Principle: Throw down a challenge.
- Carnegie method: shift attention from your own demand to the other person's dignity, desire, and point of view.

## Actionable Lessons

- Before acting, ask how the situation looks from the other person's side.
- Use this rule in one concrete sentence or behavior: Throw down a challenge.
- Keep the tone sincere, specific, and respectful.
- Watch for defensiveness; if it appears, return to appreciation, listening, or the other person's interests.
