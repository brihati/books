# 5. The Secret of Socrates

## Detailed Chapter Summary

This chapter teaches Carnegie's principle: **Get the other person saying "yes, yes" immediately.** Agreement builds momentum. Start with shared premises so the other person moves with you instead of against you. The material frames influence as an exercise in human dignity: people become more receptive when they feel understood, respected, important, and free from humiliation. The chapter's examples and anecdotes are used to turn that principle into behavior a reader can practice in ordinary conversations, sales situations, leadership moments, criticism, disagreement, and requests for cooperation.

## Key Points

- Core principle: Get the other person saying "yes, yes" immediately.
- The practical aim is to reduce defensiveness and increase willing cooperation.
- Carnegie emphasizes the other person's pride, desires, interests, and point of view as the route to influence.
- The method depends on sincerity; mechanical flattery or manipulative technique undercuts the lesson.
- The chapter is best used as a behavioral rule: apply it before trying to persuade, correct, sell, lead, or ask.

## Important Examples and Stories

- Once having said a thing, you feel you must stick to it.
- When a person says “No” and really means it, he or she is doing far more than saying a word of two letters.
- Get a student to say “No” at the beginning, or a customer, child, husband, or wife, and it takes the wisdom and the patience of angels to transform that bristling negative into an affirmative.
- The use of this “yes, yes” technique enabled James Eberson, who was a teller in the Greenwich Savings Bank, in New York City, to secure a prospective customer who might otherwise have been lost.
- My ‘yes’ response led to a purchase of the set, and when I picked it up I purchased several more items at this shop and have since become a regular customer.” Socrates, “the gadfly of Athens,” was one of the greatest philosophers the world has ever known.

## Definitions and Terms

- Principle: Get the other person saying "yes, yes" immediately.
- Carnegie method: shift attention from your own demand to the other person's dignity, desire, and point of view.

## Actionable Lessons

- Before acting, ask how the situation looks from the other person's side.
- Use this rule in one concrete sentence or behavior: Get the other person saying "yes, yes" immediately.
- Keep the tone sincere, specific, and respectful.
- Watch for defensiveness; if it appears, return to appreciation, listening, or the other person's interests.
