# Chapter 04: Step 1 - Segmentation

## Detailed Chapter Summary

Segmentation is introduced as the foundational first step in pricing strategy development. The process involves dividing a customer base into smaller, more focused groups that share specific characteristics and needs from your product category.

The chapter contrasts two primary B2B SaaS segments with distinctly different value propositions and requirements:

**Mid-Market Segment** comprises companies with 100 to 500 employees and $10M to $50M in annual revenue. These customers are price-sensitive, value simplicity and speed, and require easy implementation without heavy resource commitments.

**Enterprise Segment** consists of organizations with 5,000+ employees and $500M+ annual revenue. These buyers prioritize security, compliance, customization, and dedicated support, and are willing to invest significant time and money in solutions.

The chapter introduces the **Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)** as a detailed blueprint describing the hypothetical company that would derive the most value from your product while providing the most value to your business. Effective ICPs include specific attributes: industry, company size, annual revenue, growth stage, decision-making process, and buying triggers.

A critical warning is issued regarding **ICP Drift**—the gradual misalignment of a company's Ideal Customer Profile from its original definition. The chapter emphasizes that hidden or differing assumptions about ICP create internal confusion, lead to conflicting pricing strategy recommendations, and can waste months or years of organizational effort. Without fresh understanding and alignment on ICP definition, pricing problems cannot be effectively resolved.

## Key Points

- Segmentation requires clear understanding of customer dynamics: needs, common attributes, and product valuation methods
- Mid-market and enterprise segments have vastly different expectations despite both needing technology solutions
- An ICP is a detailed description of a hypothetical company that would get the most value from your product and provide the most value to your business
- ICP Drift undermines pricing strategy by creating confusion about target customers
- Differing assumptions about ICP lead to conflicting pricing strategy recommendations across the organization
- Company-wide agreement on 'who' the product is being sold to is essential before developing pricing strategies

## Definitions and Terms

**Segmentation:** Dividing up potential customer base into smaller, more focused groups that share specific characteristics and needs from your product category.

**ICP (Ideal Customer Profile):** A detailed description of a hypothetical company or customer that would get the most value from your product or service and provide the most value to your business; a blueprint for the type of customer you want to attract and retain.

**ICP Drift:** The gradual misalignment or deviation of a company's Ideal Customer Profile from its original definition.

**Additional key terms:** Mid-market companies, Enterprise giants, Horizontal B2B SaaS companies, Decision-Making Process, Buying Triggers, Price sensitivity.

## Actionable Lessons

1. Start pricing strategy projects by establishing clear segmentation of your customer base into focused groups
2. Create detailed Ideal Customer Profiles for each segment with specific attributes: industry, company size, revenue, growth stage, and decision-making process
3. Identify buying triggers for each segment to understand what drives purchasing decisions
4. Regularly review and align ICP definitions across teams to prevent ICP Drift
5. Ensure company-wide agreement on 'who' the product is being sold to before developing pricing strategies
6. Recognize that hidden assumptions about ICP will lead to conflicting pricing recommendations and wasted organizational effort
