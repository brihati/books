# Chapter 10, Step 5c: The Challenges of Operationalizing Pricing Strategies in SaaS

## Detailed Chapter Summary

Operationalizing pricing strategies in SaaS is inherently complex due to organizational team biases and differing perspectives. The Pricing team must act as a balancing force to align all teams around shared assumptions and goals, correcting departmental blind spots that undermine holistic business decision-making.

Different teams within an organization have distinct views on pricing rooted in their unique perspectives:

- **Product teams** overvalue features based on development effort invested rather than customer perception of value
- **Sales teams** advocate for deep discounting and lower price points, believing this accelerates sales cycles, but this perspective underestimates long-term brand and profitability impacts. Research shows no strong correlation between the level of discounting and the time it takes to close deals
- **Customer Care teams** fear increased support volume without adequate resources from lower-tier users
- **Website teams** worry about stability and performance under increased traffic from new pricing models
- **Legal teams** raise concerns about compliance and contractual implications

Operationalizing large-scale pricing changes requires structured change management to overcome organizational resistance. The ADKAR methodology—a change management framework developed by Prosci—provides a practical structure with five stages: Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, and Reinforcement.

### ADKAR Application at Citrix Online (2016)

In 2016, Citrix Online, known for GoToMeeting as a premium video conferencing solution, faced competitive pressure from Webex and Microsoft Teams. The organization undertook a major transformation introducing a Freemium package and repackaged offerings to create lower-price-point options. This initiative met significant resistance from Sales, Customer Care, and website teams.

The organization applied ADKAR methodology across all departments:

1. **Awareness**: Created through multiple communication channels—emails, town hall meetings, intranet posts—to communicate the strategic importance of pricing changes
2. **Desire**: Built by truly listening to team members across multiple formats (large meetings, smaller group discussions, one-on-one conversations) and involving all teams from the outset in decision-making
3. **Knowledge**: Developed through tailored training programs and comprehensive documentation customized for each team's specific role and needs, including FAQs, process guides, and cheat sheets
4. **Ability**: Enabled through pilot programs allowing teams to practice new roles in controlled environments before full rollout
5. **Reinforcement**: Sustained through celebrating early successes (both big and small) to build momentum, designating change champions within each team to provide ongoing coaching, and establishing continuous monitoring with feedback loops to catch issues early

Resistance was often a knee-jerk reaction when people felt overlooked or marginalized, especially if their input wasn't considered early on. By addressing each department's specific concerns with targeted solutions, the organization transformed potential resistance into support:

- Sales team was shown how easier-to-sell packages could boost their sales volume
- Customer Care team discussed strengthening support resources for increased inquiries
- Legal team concerns were mitigated through detailed assessments and reassurances
- Web team was included early to plan for site stability during transition

The structured ADKAR approach resulted in significant business outcomes: 22% increase in total revenue and almost double the number of new users from the Freemium model.

## Key Points

- Operationalizing pricing strategy in SaaS is inherently complex due to different teams' differing views on pricing stemming from unique perspectives and inherent biases
- The Pricing team must act as a balancing force to correct departmental biases and ensure holistic business decision-making
- Execution starts with alignment and diffusing team biases
- Large-scale pricing changes create resistance across multiple departments for different reasons; understanding and addressing the specific concerns of each team is critical to successful implementation
- Full alignment and agreement on assumptions and goals is required across all teams
- Change management methodology is essential for operationalizing broad re-packaging projects
- Involving teams early in decision-making transforms resistance into support
- The ADKAR framework (Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement) provides structured guidance for organizational change
- Team alignment and performance are make-or-break factors in pricing projects—often more critical than technical accuracy alone
- The Pricing team must be highly cross-functional in SaaS environments

## Definitions and Terms

- **ADKAR**: A structured change management model developed by Prosci consisting of five stages—Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement—that serves as a practical framework for individuals and organizations to successfully navigate change
- **Change champions**: Designated individuals within each team who serve as proponents of the project and maintain buy-in during implementation
- **Operationalizing pricing strategies**: The process of implementing and executing pricing decisions across the entire organization
- **Team biases**: Inherent departmental perspectives that may conflict with holistic business decision-making
- **Freemium**: A pricing model offering a free tier with paid premium features
- **Balancing force**: The Pricing team's role in correcting departmental biases and ensuring decisions reflect the entire business landscape

## Actionable Lessons

1. **Establish the Pricing team as a balancing force** across all organizational teams, with authority to correct departmental biases in service of holistic business decisions

2. **Require full alignment across teams** with explicitly agreed-upon assumptions and goals before operationalizing pricing changes

3. **Use the ADKAR change management framework** to structure organizational change:
   - Create awareness through multiple communication channels (emails, town halls, intranet posts)
   - Build desire by truly listening to team members across multiple formats (large meetings, smaller group discussions, one-on-one conversations)
   - Develop knowledge through tailored training programs customized for each team's specific role and needs
   - Enable ability through pilot programs allowing teams to practice new roles in controlled environments
   - Reinforce changes through celebrating early successes and establishing feedback loops

4. **Involve all affected teams early** in decision-making, not just to address concerns but to genuinely include them as stakeholders and decision-makers

5. **Address each department's specific concerns** with targeted solutions rather than generic approaches

6. **Develop tailored training programs** customized for each team's specific role and needs, including FAQs, process guides, and cheat sheets for easy reference

7. **Designate change champions** within each team to provide ongoing coaching and maintain buy-in during difficult implementation periods

8. **Celebrate early successes**, both big and small, to build momentum for sustained change

9. **Establish continuous monitoring and feedback loops** to catch issues early and enable course correction

10. **Recognize that team alignment and performance are more critical** to project success than technical accuracy or analytical precision alone

11. **Make pricing decisions with holistic understanding** of the entire business landscape, not through any single department's lens

12. **Ensure the Pricing team is highly cross-functional**, with representation and understanding across Sales, Product, Customer Care, Legal, and Web teams
