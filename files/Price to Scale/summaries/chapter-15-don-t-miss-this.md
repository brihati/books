# Chapter 15: Don't Miss This

## Detailed Chapter Summary

**Expansions and Renewals as a Growth Lever**

Expansions and renewals represent a historically underutilized growth lever in software go-to-market (GTM) strategies. For companies with strong customer bases, this growth avenue is worth significant strategic focus. Research from Patrick Campbell at Price Intelligently and Pacific Crest Securities demonstrates that upsells and renewals have substantially lower Customer Acquisition Costs (CAC)—approximately one-fourth the cost of new customer acquisition. More importantly, a 1% increase in monetization drives the most significant increase in profits, surpassing the impact of customer acquisition volume increases.

Despite this evidence, GTM teams (Marketing and Sales) have historically not been well-structured to prospect seamlessly into existing customers. Additionally, Customer Success teams traditionally gatekeep relationships with existing customers, creating organizational barriers to expansion revenue.

**Structuring Pricing Models for Expansion**

Successful expansion depends critically on how pricing models and package designs are constructed. Several strategic considerations emerge:

*Package Design*: Being too rigid with "good-better-best" packaging can block downstream upsell opportunities by bundling features that have been traditionally upsold or that lie unused as "shelfware" once a deal is sold. Package design directly affects the viability of future upsell and cross-sell opportunities.

*Treating Existing Customers Differently*: Existing customers may need special treatment distinct from new customer pricing. Applying the same rules to both segments can result in difficult expansion and renewal conversations. Companies with over $100M in Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) can map customer sizes to new graded plans with incentives to migrate. Creating special upsell bundles or granular feature menus only for current customers can ease natural transitions.

*Volume and Usage Management*: Volume upsells are often missed in early GTM stages. Product instrumentation is critical to meter usage for overage pricing. Upsells happen naturally as a consequence of increased customer needs rather than multi-channel marketing pushes. When accounts exceed usage expectations, systems must flag upsell motions automatically.

*Cannibalization Risk*: Pricing model redesigns carry significant risk. Introducing commoditization responses, downmarket entry pricing, or selling the same product at heavily discounted wholesale prices via channel partners creates cannibalization risk—existing customers may churn from higher margin plans if perceiving better deals elsewhere. Customers will be less likely to want to downgrade if they perceive new plans as substantially different from what they currently have. List price increases of 2-3x for existing customers requiring premium plan upgrades can cause customer resistance and churn.

**Cross-Sells, Usage Clauses, and Bundling**

Contract language plays a critical role in expansion revenue. Usage clauses must be explicit in all new client and renewal contracts with agreed-upon overage rates. Not having this language is bound to cause pain where every renewal opportunity becomes a challenging negotiation. Without proper metering, companies forgo revenue and increase churn risk.

Cross-sells should receive special strategic treatment. Larger companies like Salesforce and Zendesk lock in customers through multi-product deals with steep discounts; smaller companies must employ the same strategy. Cross-sells should receive special discounting and payment terms to make competitive RFPs (Request for Proposals) unnecessary. A distinct discounting and approval process for cross-sells should be separate from new business deal closure processes.

Bundling multiple products or features together at discounted rates is often considered a "silver bullet" for driving revenue and decreasing churn through multi-product purchasing. Bundling can increase perceived value and Average Revenue Per User (ARPU), but it risks customer overwhelm and reduced pricing flexibility for future upsells and cross-sells. Bundling must be carefully designed to align with customer needs and not create unnecessary complexity.

**Organizational Alignment**

As a maturing SaaS company approaches 50% of revenue generated from renewals, Customer Success should rise in strategic importance to the business. The VP of Customer Success is a key stakeholder for cross-sell and renewal strategies, equal in importance to the VP of Sales. GTM teams must establish structures that can prospect seamlessly into existing customers, with Account Managers ("farmers") serving as counterparts to New Business "hunters" in larger organizations. Marketing, Sales, and Customer Success teams must align to enable expansion revenue.

## Important Examples and Stories

- **Salesforce and Zendesk**: Use multi-product deals with steep discounts to lock clients into their ecosystems
- **Account Manager model**: In larger organizations, Account Managers serve as "farmers" (expansion-focused) counterparts to New Business "hunters" (acquisition-focused)
- **Customer migration strategy**: Companies with >$100M ARR can map customer sizes to new graded plans with incentives to migrate, treating migration as its own project separate from initial pricing rollout
- **Custom bundling for existing customers**: Creating special upsell bundles or granular feature menus only for current customers eases natural transitions
- **Shelfware problem**: Features bundled into packages after deal closure that go unused, blocking future upsell opportunities
- **Cannibalization through channel partners**: Selling the same product via channel partners at heavily discounted wholesale prices causes existing customer churn to lower-cost options

## Definitions and Terms

- **Expansions and renewals**: Growth lever focused on upselling and renewing existing customer relationships
- **Customer Acquisition Costs (CAC)**: Cost to acquire new customers; expansions and renewals have substantially lower CAC
- **Account Manager**: "Farmer" role in larger organizations, counterpart to New Business "hunters"; responsible for customer expansion
- **Shelfware**: Unused features bundled into a package after a deal is sold
- **Overage pricing**: Charging for usage exceeding contracted amounts to incentivize moves to higher value tiers
- **Cannibalization**: Existing customers churning from higher margin plans to lower margin alternatives
- **Bundling**: Offering multiple products or features together at discounted rates to increase perceived value and promote multi-product purchasing
- **Go-To-Market (GTM)**: Strategy for acquiring and expanding customer relationships; includes Marketing, Sales, and GTM operations
- **Customer Success (CS)**: Team responsible for customer retention, expansion, and satisfaction post-sale
- **ARPU (Average Revenue Per User)**: Average revenue generated per user; bundling can increase this metric
- **RFP (Request for Proposal)**: Formal customer request for pricing on competitive solutions; proper cross-sell discounting prevents need for competitive RFPs

## Actionable Lessons

1. **Establish GTM structures** that can prospect seamlessly into existing customers; create distinct Account Manager ("farmer") roles for expansion work
2. **Prioritize monetization improvements** alongside new customer acquisition; track that 1% monetization gains drive greater profit increases than volume gains
3. **Design pricing packages carefully** to avoid bundling features that have been traditionally upsold or that create shelfware
4. **Create special upsell bundles or granular feature menus** only for current customers to ease natural transitions without overwhelming them
5. **Map customer sizes to new graded plans** with migration incentives for large customer bases (companies >$100M ARR)
6. **Use different nomenclature and service offerings** to make new plans feel distinct and prevent downgrades from higher to lower margin tiers
7. **Put explicit usage clause language** in all new client and renewal contracts with agreed-upon overage rates; ensure internal mechanisms flag upsell motions when accounts exceed usage expectations
8. **Instrument products properly** to meter usage and identify overage opportunities automatically
9. **Offer special discounts and payment terms** on cross-sells to existing clients to prevent competitive RFPs
10. **Establish distinct discounting and approval processes** for cross-sells separate from new business deal closure
11. **Elevate VP of Customer Success** as a key stakeholder equal to VP of Sales for cross-sell and renewal pricing policies, especially as renewal revenue approaches 50% of total revenue
12. **Align Marketing, Sales, and Customer Success teams** to enable expansion revenue
13. **Treat customer migration to new pricing** as its own project separate from initial pricing rollout for major changes
14. **Provide explicit no-downgrade policies** where existing customers are not shown lower list prices than currently paying
