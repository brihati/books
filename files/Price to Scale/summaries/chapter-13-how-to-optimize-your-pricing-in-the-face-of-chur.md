# Chapter 13: How To Optimize Your Pricing in the Face of Churn & Inflation in SaaS

## Detailed Chapter Summary

This chapter addresses the critical intersection of pricing optimization, economic conditions, and customer retention in SaaS businesses. The core argument centers on a counterintuitive insight: **regime changes driven by interest rate shifts—not inflation itself—are the primary drivers of SaaS pricing dynamics**.

### The Regime Change Framework

The chapter establishes that inflation impacts industries asymmetrically. While healthcare services and college tuition have increased significantly, technology products have become more affordable. This divergence occurs because SaaS faces **software deflation** through commoditization: unique features that once commanded premium prices become standard offerings available from many competitors at lower prices. The example of **DocuSign** illustrates this pattern—when competitors like **PandaDoc** and **HelloSign** entered the market with similar services at lower prices, DocuSign faced market saturation, price wars, and feature parity.

SaaS valuations are highly sensitive to interest rate changes, with lower rates encouraging investment and higher valuations. The relationship between the 10-year treasury rate (tracked from January 2000 to June 2022) and company valuations demonstrates this correlation. As interest rates rose from 2022-24, companies shifted focus toward operational efficiency and margin improvement, moving away from growth-at-all-costs strategies.

### The Double-Sided Challenge

Companies face conflicting pressures: they must raise pricing to increase free cash flow (FCF), yet simultaneously face churn pressure from reducing net revenue retention (NRR). Median software NRR reached its lowest point since 2020 by mid-2024, indicating increased churn as customers scrutinize software spend. This creates a fundamental tension—simple price increases are unlikely to succeed in this environment.

### Strategic Solutions: Packaging and Pricing Metrics

Rather than relying on outright price increases, the chapter advocates for two primary strategies:

**Packaging Strategy**: Move from one-size-fits-all offerings to granular options. The shift from 2021 (when simplicity drove deal velocity) to 2024 (when granular/flexible pricing increases deal sizes) reflects changing market conditions. Specific approaches include:

- **Lite packages** for defensive retention of at-risk customers
- **A-la-carte models** for enterprise segments with custom scoping to increase Average Deal Sizes
- **Unbundling high-cost capabilities** like SMS messaging and data storage from $/user pricing into separate cost-based metrics

**Pricing Metric Optimization**: Change pricing metrics when misaligned with customer ROI or growth trajectory. If usage isn't proportional to customer business growth, the link to ROI breaks and churn increases. Examples include $/MAU (monthly active users) or $/customer visits as better alignment vehicles than simple usage-based metrics in some contexts. Co-pilots, for instance, can use $/execution or $/completion metrics alongside base $/user platform fees.

### Hierarchy of Pricing Actions

The chapter establishes a priority order: packaging optimization and metric changes should precede outright price increases on existing customers. Existing customers perceive forced annual pricing increases differently than new customers having the option to choose—the former represents a significant customer dissatisfaction risk.

## Actionable Lessons

1. **Reconsider pricing strategies based on regime changes driven by interest rates rather than general inflation metrics**

2. **Move packaging toward a-la-carte options to monetize currently unmonetized product areas with market demand**

3. **Review existing packages for inefficiencies where capabilities valued by smaller segments are bundled but could be upsells**

4. **Create lite package options to defensively retain at-risk customers rather than losing them completely**

5. **Unbundle high-cost capabilities (SMS, data storage, AI components) into separate cost-based pricing metrics rather than bundling into $/user pricing**

6. **Evaluate whether current pricing metrics are proportional to customer business growth and ROI alignment**

7. **Use a-la-carte or custom scoping for enterprise segments with whale behavior to increase Average Deal Sizes**

8. **Implement metric changes before considering outright price increases on existing customers**

9. **Focus on operational efficiency improvements and margin optimization in response to higher interest rates**

10. **Monitor revenue per employee metrics as a key indicator of productivity and efficiency**

11. **Prepare for increased churn and scrutiny of software spend as companies tighten budgets in higher interest rate environments**
