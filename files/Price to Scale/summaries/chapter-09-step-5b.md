# Chapter 09: Step 5b – Deal Desk Function and Deal Structuring

## Detailed Chapter Summary

Step 5b addresses the critical infrastructure needed to scale pricing strategy through the establishment and evolution of a Deal Desk (DD) function. This chapter explains why companies need a Deal Desk, how it operates as a strategic partner to sales, and the specific mechanics of deal structuring through discounting and contract terms.

### The Role and Evolution of Deal Desk

The Deal Desk is responsible for reviewing and approving pricing and deal structure, ensuring compliance with price structure, profitability, and solution components. Its role is evolving from a purely transactional function to becoming a strategic partner to sales, driving deals and decisions. As Aunik Bhattacharjee observed when joining Elastic, a sales-driven organization without a Deal Desk function results in "things getting lost in translation, whether that be data, whether that be translation of contract terms, especially those ones that are nonstandard for your more strategic customers."

The Deal Desk operates as "Air Traffic Control for Deals," ensuring smooth information flow and timely deal closure. This function becomes critical as organizations scale toward IPO or exit events, where process standardization and compliance requirements intensify.

### Comparison: With and Without a Deal Desk

**Without Deal Desk:**
- Decentralized, inconsistent proposals
- Ad-hoc discounting policies with no controls
- Non-compliant contracts lacking legal vetting
- Limited operational visibility into deal progression
- Subjective, inconsistent post-deal analysis and lessons learned

**With Deal Desk:**
- Uniform proposals generated via CRM integration
- High win rates achieved at speed with controlled discounting
- Legal-vetted contracts using automated templates
- Comprehensive dashboards providing real-time visibility
- Data-driven post-deal analysis enabling continuous improvement

### Deal Structuring: Discounting and Contract Terms

Deal structuring requires managing two critical components: discounting types and contract terms that define the service delivery lifecycle and set clear expectations for both parties.

**Discounting Levers:**
- **Volume-Based Discounts:** Reduced prices for bulk purchases, encouraging larger orders and increasing revenue
- **Early Payment Discounts:** Incentives for customers to pay invoices early, improving cash flow and financial stability
- **Bundling Discounts:** Selling multiple products or services together at a lower price, increasing perceived value and promoting new products

**Essential Contract Terms:**
- **Roles and Responsibilities:** Clarity on what each party delivers and maintains
- **Service Specifications:** Detailed descriptions of service features and capabilities
- **Usage Guidelines:** Data management policies and compliance with GDPR and HIPAA
- **Payment Terms:** Specified as Net30, Net60, or milestone-based payments aligned with project progress
- **Termination Conditions:** Exit strategies offering flexibility for both parties
- **Indemnity Clauses:** Protection against software errors and regulatory fines (particularly important for financial services)
- **Liability Limitations:** Caps on liability exposure, negotiated based on customer impact risk
- **Confidentiality Agreements:** Protection of proprietary technology and information
- **Service Level Agreements (SLAs):** Performance standards and compensation for unmet standards
- **Dispute Resolution Mechanisms:** Processes for handling disagreements
- **Compliance with Laws and Regulations:** Adherence to applicable legal frameworks
- **Force Majeure Clauses:** Protection against unforeseen circumstances

### Real-World Examples

Deal structuring varies significantly by customer type and industry:
- A **small business** negotiates payment terms from Net30 to Net60 to align with cash flow cycles
- A **healthcare provider** negotiates customized reporting tools and medical device integration for their EHR system
- A **financial services company** insists on robust indemnity clauses against software errors and regulatory fines
- A **large enterprise** negotiates higher liability caps reflecting the significant potential impact of software failures
- A **tech startup** emphasizes strict confidentiality agreements to protect innovative technology
- A **company heavily reliant on CRM** negotiates stricter SLAs with significant penalties for non-compliance

### Key Stakeholders and Competencies

The Deal Desk function requires involvement of key stakeholders: sales, finance, and pricing teams, ensuring compliance and consistency in sales policies and pricing contract terms. A primary competency for Deal Desk teams is "taking the number of deals that you do and standardizing a larger percentage of them as you scale your business."

### Performance Analytics and Continuous Improvement

The Deal Desk generates analytics on key performance indicators including win rates, deal cycle times, and revenue impacts. These metrics enable performance analytics and continuous improvement across revenue organizations, supporting the shift from transactional to strategic operations.

## Actionable Lessons

1. **Establish a Deal Desk Function:** Create a dedicated Deal Desk to manage complex sales processes with bespoke pricing and customized terms, particularly as you approach scale or exit events

2. **Implement Centralized Proposal Generation:** Use CRM integration to generate uniform proposals, ensuring consistency and efficient generation across all deals

3. **Define Clear Discounting Policies:** Set explicit policies around discounting (volume-based, early payment, bundling) with defined approval flows and authority levels

4. **Use Legal-Vetted Templates:** Create and maintain automated contract templates pre-vetted by legal departments to ensure consistency and legal robustness

5. **Create Real-Time Visibility:** Develop comprehensive dashboards to track all deal stages, enabling quick identification of bottlenecks and miscommunications

6. **Conduct Structured Post-Deal Analysis:** Implement quantitative and qualitative data collection to drive data-driven post-deal reviews and identify lessons learned

7. **Clarify Roles and Responsibilities:** Explicitly outline each party's roles and responsibilities in contracts to prevent misunderstandings and disputes

8. **Include Compliance-Critical Terms:** Ensure contracts incorporate usage guidelines covering data management policies and compliance with GDPR, HIPAA, and other applicable regulations

9. **Specify Payment Terms Clearly:** Define payment terms (Net30, Net60, milestone-based) aligned with project progress and customer cash flow considerations

10. **Define Termination Conditions:** Include clear termination conditions offering exit strategies for both parties, balancing stability with flexibility

11. **Ensure Multi-Functional Involvement:** Involve sales, finance, and pricing teams in Deal Desk decisions to ensure compliance, consistency, and alignment across organization

12. **Standardize as You Scale:** Prioritize standardizing a larger percentage of deals as the organization scales to improve efficiency, reduce errors, and maintain pricing discipline

13. **Track Key Performance Indicators:** Monitor win rates, deal cycle times, and revenue impacts to identify improvement opportunities and measure Deal Desk effectiveness

14. **Evolve from Transactional to Strategic:** Build the Deal Desk's capability to move beyond compliance and approval toward strategic partnership with sales, actively driving deals and decisions
