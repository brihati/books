# Chapter 22: Index

## Detailed Chapter Summary

This chapter is a comprehensive alphabetical index to *The Strategy and Tactics of Pricing*, organizing key concepts, companies, authors, legal cases, and page references discussed throughout the book. The index serves as a reference tool for locating pricing strategy frameworks, methodologies, case examples, and legal precedents.

### Core Pricing Frameworks and Methodologies

The index references several foundational pricing approaches:

- **Economic Value Estimation (EVE) Model** (pages 27, 46–47): A framework for determining customer value by comparing offerings to the next-best competitive alternative (NBCA)
- **Customer Value Modeling (CVM)** (pages 46–47): Distinguished from the EVE model as an alternative valuation approach
- **Value Cascade** (pages 11, 27): Strategic pricing concept linking economic value to pricing decisions
- **Conjoint (Trade-off) Analysis** (pages 192–197): Quantitative technique for measuring price sensitivity and buyer preferences, illustrated through the Blue Sky Ski Company example (pages 194–197)

## Key Points

1. **Strategic pricing requires integrated frameworks** combining value estimation (EVE model), segmentation, and competitive analysis
2. **Price sensitivity measurement** employs multiple techniques—actual purchases, surveys, conjoint analysis, and panel data—each suited to different contexts
3. **Price structures** use fences (buyer identification, timing, quantity, location) to implement differentiated pricing while maintaining fairness
4. **Organizational capability** requires decision rights clarity, cross-functional coordination, performance alignment, and systems support
5. **Legal compliance** is mandatory under Robinson-Patman Act, Sherman Act, and FTC regulations, requiring cost justification for price discrimination
6. **Psychological value drivers**—switching costs, fairness effects, reference prices—significantly influence price sensitivity beyond economic value
7. **Power buyer management** requires quantifying value, eliminating unnecessary costs, and transparent segmentation rather than reactive discounting
8. **Pricing policy** should transition from flexible, ad hoc negotiation to consistent, transparent, rule-based approaches
9. **Specialized strategies** adapt pricing over product life cycles—innovation, growth, maturity, and decline—with distinct approaches
10. **Financial analysis** must distinguish incremental from sunk costs and use breakeven analysis to evaluate pricing decisions
