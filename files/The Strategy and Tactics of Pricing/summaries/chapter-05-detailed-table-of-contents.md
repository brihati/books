# Chapter 05: Detailed Table of Contents

## Detailed Chapter Summary

This chapter presents a comprehensive structural overview of a pricing strategy book organized into 11 chapters and appendices. The content spans from foundational pricing concepts through specialized applications and organizational implementation.

### Strategic Foundations (Chapters 1-3)
The book begins with strategic pricing fundamentals, defining strategic pricing as "coordinating the drivers of profitability and leveraging profit into sustainable growth." Economic value serves as the guiding force throughout pricing decisions. The framework addresses three core pricing approaches: cost-plus pricing, customer-driven pricing, and share-driven pricing.

### Price Structure and Policy (Chapters 4-5)
Price structure encompasses tactics for pricing differently across customer segments through value-based market segmentation—a six-step process including determining segmentation criteria, identifying discriminating value drivers, determining operational constraints, creating segments, developing descriptions, and creating metrics and fences. Price fences are implemented through four mechanisms: buyer identification fences, purchase location fences, time-of-purchase fences, and purchase quantity fences. Pricing policy influences customer expectations and purchase behaviors, with nine strategies for conveying value and segment-specific bundle development.

### Price Competition and Measurement (Chapters 6-7)
Price competition requires thoughtful management of conflict, including proactive, policy-based price negotiation rather than reactive, ad hoc approaches. Price sensitivity measurement uses research techniques—historical data, experiments, and surveys—to supplement judgment. A five-step price-setting process is recommended: define viable price range, make strategic choices, assess breakeven sales changes, gauge price elasticity, and account for psychological factors.

### Financial and Specialized Strategies (Chapters 8-9)
Financial analysis focuses on incremental and avoidable costs for pricing decisions rather than full absorption costing. Specialized strategies adapt pricing across product life cycles from innovation through maturity, including peak pricing, yield management, and performance-based metrics. Four generic strategies manage exchange rate price adjustments.

### Organizational Capability (Chapters 10-11)
Pricing organization requires three essential elements: talent, processes, and data. Pricing objectives must align across the organization with clear decision rights specifying roles and responsibilities. Performance measures and incentives should align sales with strategy. Customer profitability analytics examine cost to serve and win-loss data.

### Legal and Ethical Constraints
Legal considerations include price fixing (horizontal, resale, and vertical), price discrimination (promotional discrimination and competitive injury), predatory pricing, and price signaling. Sarbanes-Oxley affects pricing practices. Vertical non-price restrictions and direct dealing programs provide alternative approaches to pricing constraints.
