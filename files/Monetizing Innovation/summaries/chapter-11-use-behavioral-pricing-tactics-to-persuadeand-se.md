# CHAPTER 11: Use Behavioral Pricing Tactics to Persuade and Sell

## Detailed Chapter Summary

Behavioral pricing uses psychological tactics to influence customer purchasing decisions beyond rational value analysis. The chapter demonstrates that customers make decisions based on both rational and emotional factors, and that pricing strategies ignoring the irrational side of customer psychology are likely suboptimal.

### Foundation: Behavioral Economics and Irrational Decision-Making

Behavioral economics, developed by researchers including Daniel Kahneman, Amos Tversky, Richard Thaler, and Dan Ariely (1970s-80s), recognizes that buyers are not always rational actors. Willingness to pay is not solely based on product value but also psychological factors. A classic example illustrates this irrationality: people are willing to pay twice as much for beer from a resort versus a run-down store despite the product being identical. "From an economic perspective, this makes no sense; it is, in a word, irrational."

### Core Concept: Behavioral Pricing Definition

**Behavioral pricing** is defined as "pricing tactics that play to the irrational side of customers by refining product offers and messages to make it easier for customers to compare, decide, and purchase." The chapter states: "Behavioral pricing is the magic that happens when value pricing meets irrational customer psychology."

### Case Study: Internet Start-up Transformation (2012)

An Internet start-up company demonstrated the power of behavioral pricing through a four-tier product restructuring:

**Initial State:** Company with 'good, better, best' product lineup where 60% of customers selected the entry-level offering.

**Discovery:** Psychological price thresholds existed at $49, $99, and $199.

**Implementation:** The company:
- Reduced Basic functionality
- Increased Standard from $79 to $99
- Increased Advanced from $149 to $199
- Created a new Premium tier at $299

**Results:** 
- 36% ARPU (average revenue per user) increase
- 29% MRR (monthly recurring revenue) increase from new customers
- 14 percentage points of the 29% MRR increase attributed to behavioral pricing
- 15 percentage points attributed to product configuration changes

This case demonstrates that behavioral pricing requires little additional cost, making revenue increases go directly to profit. Multibillion-dollar companies typically see 1-3% sales boosts from behavioral pricing, while start-ups can achieve much higher gains.

### Six Behavioral Pricing Tactics

#### 1. **Anchor Products and Price Anchoring**

**Definition:** Offers promoted to make another product look better by providing a reference point for comparison.

**Mechanism:** Premium anchor products make standard and advanced products appear as bargains. The Premium offering at $299 in the start-up case was "the anchor, the Internet start-up company's version of the movie theater's small soda. The offering's sole purpose was to make the other products look like bargains."

**Examples:**
- Movie theater concession stand: large soda priced at $5.99 versus small soda at $4.99 uses price anchoring to make the large seem like a bargain
- The Economist magazine A/B pricing experiment (2012): 84% chose the print/online bundle in group B (with $125 print-only anchor) versus 32% in group A (without anchor). The online-only subscription was $59, and the print-and-online combination was $125.
- Wine store example: customers choosing between $10, $25, and $40 bottles typically select the $25 middle option

#### 2. **Compromise Effect**

**Definition:** When given a set of choices, people avoid extremes and tend to select the middle option.

**Application:** Plan on having a compromise option when launching a new product. This tactic works because customers naturally gravitate toward middle-ground choices.

#### 3. **Price-Quality Signaling**

**Definition:** Using price to reinforce customer perception of quality (low price = low quality; high price = high quality).

**Key Principle:** "If it costs more, it reinforces the customer's perception of quality." Conversely, "Low price equals low quality; high price equals high quality."

**Evidence:** A placebo study by Ariely (2008) showed that 85% of participants reported pain reduction at a $2.50 pill price, while only 61% reported pain reduction at a 10-cent pill price—despite the pills being identical. "The price—not the pill—relieved the pain."

**Example:** iPhone launch in 2007 at $599 to signal quality. "If Apple's Steve Jobs had debuted the iPhone at $49, it would have been a terrible mistake." A $49 price would have undercut competitors but depressed smartphone prices industry-wide.

**Critical Warning:** "Pricing your product too low is worse than pricing it too high. If you start high you can still go down; if you start low you can hardly go up."

#### 4. **Razor/Razor Blades Model**

**Definition:** Low upfront costs with higher variable costs over time.

**Mechanism:** Customers are more influenced by upfront costs than total cost of ownership. "Customers are influenced by costs that are immediately in front of them. The customer's upfront cost has a much bigger psychological impact than the total cost of ownership."

**Examples:**
- Coffee machine pricing: Option A ($480 machine, $10/lb coffee) versus Option B ($120 machine, $40/lb coffee)—both cost the same over 12 months but Option B performs better due to lower upfront cost
- Razor companies: low upfront investment for razors but higher investment in razor blades over time
- Computer printer manufacturers: low upfront investment but expensive ink cartridges
- Computer hardware manufacturers: sell cheaper products but make more money on services

**Critical Caveat:** Only use this tactic if 100% certain you can sell downstream products to customers.

#### 5. **Pennies-a-Day Pricing**

**Definition:** Breaking down annual contract prices into monthly installments to reduce sticker shock.

**Mechanism:** Framing prices in smaller units (hourly, monthly, pennies-a-day) reduces sticker shock and customer resistance.

**Examples:**
- Amazon EC2 quotes prices in dollars or fractions of a penny for hourly prices instead of hundreds or thousands per server
- Online subscription company: frame $120 annual contract as $9.99 monthly payment

#### 6. **Psychological Price Thresholds**

**Definition:** Limits to what most customers will spend depending on product configuration. Price points where customer acceptance drops significantly.

**Key Insight:** "You rarely see prices like $101 in retail; you almost always see $99 or $99.99." The perceived difference between $99.99 and $101 is more than a dollar and a penny.

**Evidence:** Online subscription company study showed price thresholds at $40, $70, and $100. When pricing at $69.99 versus $71, more than 20% of customers rejected the $71 price while accepting $69.99. "This company was much better off pricing its product at $69.99 than at $71."

### Integration and Testing

**Combining Approaches:** "Combining rational and behavioral pricing approaches is the most effective strategy." Neither approach alone is sufficient.

**Testing Methods:**
1. **Focus groups:** Reveal when tactics like premium product anchors create decision paralysis
2. **A/B testing:** For online offers to get statistically significant data on conversion rates and click-through rates
3. **Large-scale experiments or offline surveys:** When price transparency requirements prevent live testing

**Balance:** "If you use too many tactics, you will confuse customers. If you use too few of them, you won't fully monetize your new product."

## Key Points

1. Behavioral pricing addresses the irrational side of customer psychology, complementing rational value-based pricing
2. Price anchors make expensive options appear more attractive by comparison
3. Customers have psychological price thresholds that limit spending at specific price points
4. Price signals quality; low prices suggest low quality, high prices suggest high quality
5. Upfront costs have greater psychological impact than total cost of ownership
6. Breaking down large prices into smaller units reduces sticker shock
7. Behavioral pricing applies to both B2B and consumer markets because you are selling to people
8. Behavioral pricing requires little additional cost, making revenue increases go directly to profit
9. Testing is essential before implementing behavioral pricing tactics
10. Combining rational and behavioral pricing approaches is most effective

## Definitions and Terms

- **Behavioral pricing:** Pricing tactics that play to the irrational side of customers by refining product offers and messages to make it easier for customers to compare, decide, and purchase
- **Price anchors:** Offers promoted to make another product look better by providing a reference point for comparison
- **Psychological price thresholds:** Limits to what most customers will spend depending on product configuration
- **Compromise effect:** When given a set of choices, people avoid extremes and tend to select the middle option
- **Price-quality signaling:** Using price to reinforce customer perception of quality
- **Razor/razor blades model:** Low upfront costs with higher variable costs over time
- **Pennies-a-day pricing:** Breaking down annual contract prices into smaller time units to reduce sticker shock
- **Price cliff:** A price point where more than 20% of customers reject the offer
- **ARPU (average revenue per user):** Average revenue generated per customer
- **MRR (monthly recurring revenue):** Total recurring revenue generated monthly
- **Behavioral economics:** Field recognizing that buyers are not always rational actors
- **Willingness to pay (WTP):** Customer's maximum price they will pay for a product
- **Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS):** Cloud computing services where customers rent computing capacity for finite amounts of time
- **Elastic Compute Cloud (EC2):** Amazon's service allowing customers to rent computing capacity and pay only when they need it

## Actionable Lessons

### Strategic Pricing Development
1. Identify psychological price thresholds for your customer segments to optimize pricing
2. Identify price thresholds for your products and position pricing just below them to avoid price cliffs
3. Beware of too-low pricing when launching new products as it can ruin quality perception
4. Start B2B sales negotiations for new products with a high anchor price
5. Combine rational and behavioral pricing approaches rather than relying solely on behavioral techniques

### Product Tiering and Anchoring
6. Plan on having a compromise option when launching a new product
7. Make sure you have an anchor product in your new product offering portfolio
8. Reduce functionality in entry-level offerings to avoid making higher-priced tiers less attractive
9. Redistribute features across product tiers to make each tier more compelling
10. Consider creating ultrapremium offerings to capture additional revenue

### Pricing Presentation
11. Use price anchors strategically to make premium offerings appear more attractive
