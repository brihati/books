The mixing and blending of cultural heritages will create new hybrid groups and identities, such as “tribal tech” and “industrial chic.” People will form groups or “tribes” in other ways as a reaction to the fear of the harsh, unpredictable realities of the outside world. Cocooning will continue to be important, along with the accoutrements that support it— everything from cozy blankets to products and services that bring us closer to safety and elicit trust and comfort. Anxiety-ridden by simultaneous social, economic, political, and ethical chaos, some people will find themselves stretched beyond their ability to

cope with today or imagine tomorrow. The search for purpose, meaning, and spiritual connection will become even more pronounced. The search to find like-minded people, especially those that offer spiritual and emotional sustenance and protection from unfriendly forces and persecution (real or imagined), will open up new realms for aesthetic innovation as well. New products and services that acknowledge and celebrate religious faith and spirituality will find new and nontraditional markets for their offerings. Venxara’s Spiritual Warriors Couture 27 line exemplifies this, with its handbags, totes, and jewelry adorned with artwork featuring various Catholic saints. You’ll also see a growth in modest fashion prompted by and geared toward women of Muslim, Orthodox Jewish, and other religious faiths. The new modesty will find its way into mainstream fashion, and many people will gravitate toward it for the same reasons they will gravitate toward spirituality—for a sense of comfort and protection from the hostile outside world. As communities focus on spiritual growth and renewal, expect a sharper focus on purpose, meaning, and making a positive difference. Expect intense focus on long-term sustainability of our planet. There will also be nostalgia for earlier generations’ seemingly carefree childhoods. Baby boomers will find comfort in familiar pursuits and products from their youth, as will millennials and Gen Xers. Retrograde brands such as Pabst and Schlitz (beer), Polaroid (cameras), Schwinn (bicycles), Keds (sneakers), Fresca (soda), Shalimar (perfume), Arthur Treacher’s Fish & Chips (fast food), Hydrox (cookies), and Fiorucci (fashion) will be back in demand. New brands that can successfully evoke nostalgic feelings of a more innocent and insular time also will win. Secession into tribes or cultural/value groups will affect the beauty industry as well. There will be a shift from the idealization of youth to a focus on healthy, happy, intelligent aging. This is happening already, through recent advertisements from the beauty chain store Ulta Beauty. In 2016 and through part of 2018, Ulta’s television and print ads had a cartoonlike quality, presenting women as perfect, doll-like beings. Despite there being a variety of ethnicities represented, they all looked pretty much the same. Today, Ulta’s branding and marketing celebrate beauty that comes in all shapes and sizes, a welcome message that is helping the company succeed in a variety of markets. It’s also one of the few retailers to sell high-end and low-end products side by side. 28 It sells joy, not youth—and as a result, the company’s stock price grew by more than 3,000 percent between 2009 and 2016, outperforming the S&P 500, which grew only 250 percent during the same period. 29 The beauty sector will become more diverse, making room for products designed for those with special needs and ailments, recognizing the beauty in all forms of the human condition. Blurred Lines As I said earlier, people will form groups driven by shared ideologies, interests, and beliefs, but oftentimes groups and group members will identify outside of conventional norms. There is already a blurring of lines happening between male and female; straight and gay; black and white; and young and old. 30 As a result, more brands and categories once conventionally divided by gender or age will become unisex or offer unisex items and age-fluid products and services. The children’s brand Primary 31 offers basics such as T-shirts, leggings, pants, skirts, and dresses in bright solid colors meant to be worn by all children from ages zero to twelve; conventional boys (pants, Ts) and girls (dresses, skirts) items are marketed to all children. The Phluid Project in SoHo in New York City may be the first officially gender-free retail space in the world. At three thousand square feet, the bright white store with large windows and high ceilings is part retail space and part “experiential platform,” according to its content director, Jillian Brooks. 32 The store, aimed toward gender-nonconforming and gender-fluid consumers, uses customized gender-free mannequins that show off unisex basics from brands such as Levi’s and Soulland, along with more fashion- forward choices from Gypsy Sport, Skingraft, and the fetish-inspired latex by Meat.
