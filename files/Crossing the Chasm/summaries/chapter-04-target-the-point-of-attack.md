# Target the Point of Attack

## Detailed Chapter Summary

This chapter focuses on selecting the beachhead through target-customer scenarios rather than abstract segmentation. The right target is a concrete buyer context with a compelling reason to buy, identifiable economic and technical buyers, a reachable user community, and a pain strong enough to overcome pragmatist caution. Moore stresses that early targeting is decision-making under uncertainty, so the goal is disciplined focus rather than perfect information.

## Key Points

- A target customer is more useful than a broad target segment because it forces concrete assumptions.
- The beachhead should have urgent pain, reachable buyers, and a path to references.
- Scenario work helps compare segments when hard data is incomplete.
- The selected niche should be narrow enough to win and meaningful enough to matter.

## Important Examples And Stories

- Target-customer scenarios force the team to specify users, buyers, pain, workflow, and buying process.
- The chapter treats segmentation as a practical choice under uncertainty rather than a purely analytical exercise.
