# Assemble the Invasion Force

## Detailed Chapter Summary

This chapter develops the whole-product requirement. Pragmatists do not buy an isolated core product; they buy a complete solution that fits their workflow, infrastructure, support expectations, and risk constraints. Crossing the chasm therefore requires assembling partners, services, integrations, support, and commitments around the beachhead customer. The work is not just product management; it is ecosystem design for a specific market promise.

## Key Points

- The whole product is the gap between the shipped product and the complete solution the customer expects.
- Visionaries may help assemble missing pieces; pragmatists expect the vendor ecosystem to do it.
- Partners and alliances matter when they complete the offer for the target niche.
- The whole-product plan should be scoped to the beachhead rather than to every possible market.

## Important Examples And Stories

- The whole-product model distinguishes the generic product from the full solution required for mainstream adoption.
- The chapter uses partner and alliance planning to show how a vendor can complete the offer for a specific niche.
