# Define the Battle

## Detailed Chapter Summary

This chapter covers competition and positioning. The company must define the category, the alternative, the target buyer, and the reason to believe in a way pragmatists can understand. In the chasm period, the goal is to create the perception of market leadership inside a manageable niche, even if the broader category is still forming. Positioning must reduce risk for the buyer and make the product easy to compare against the status quo or a known alternative.

## Key Points

- Competition includes the current way the target customer solves the problem, not only direct product rivals.
- Positioning should identify the target customer, problem, product category, key benefit, and proof.
- A focused niche lets a company credibly claim leadership before it can lead the whole market.
- The message should fit mainstream buying criteria rather than early-market excitement.

## Important Examples And Stories

- The positioning work compares the new offer against the buyer’s current alternative, not just another startup.
- The niche-leadership logic shows how a company can create mainstream credibility before it has broad-market dominance.
