# Introduction

## Detailed Chapter Summary

The introduction frames the central problem: a high-tech product can win enthusiasm in an early market and still stall before the mainstream market. The risky transition is the chasm between visionary early adopters and pragmatist buyers, whose buying criteria, reference needs, and tolerance for incomplete products are fundamentally different. The practical implication is that a company must diagnose its place in the adoption life cycle before choosing its go-to-market strategy.

## Key Points

- The early market and mainstream market behave like different markets, not a smooth continuum.
- Visionaries can sponsor change before a complete solution exists; pragmatists usually wait for references, standards, and proof.
- Crossing the chasm requires changing strategy, not simply pushing harder with the same early-market tactics.

## Important Examples And Stories

- The introduction contrasts early-market momentum with the later stall that appears when mainstream buyers do not share the same references or risk appetite.
- The fad-versus-trend framing shows why early excitement can fail to become durable adoption.
