# High-Tech Marketing Illusion

## Detailed Chapter Summary

This chapter explains the technology adoption life cycle and the illusion that success flows naturally from innovators to early adopters, early majority, late majority, and laggards. Moore emphasizes the gaps between adopter groups, especially the chasm between visionaries and pragmatists. Each group buys for different reasons, relies on different references, and expects different levels of product completeness. The mistake is treating the market as continuous when the sales motion, proof, and product expectations must change.

## Key Points

- The adoption curve is useful only if its discontinuities are taken seriously.
- Innovators and visionaries tolerate risk and incompleteness; pragmatists require reliability, references, and a whole product.
- The chasm is created by the lack of credible references and shared buying criteria between early adopters and mainstream buyers.
- High-tech companies often misread early-market excitement as evidence of mainstream demand.

## Important Examples And Stories

- The chapter uses the adoption curve to separate innovators, early adopters, early majority, late majority, and laggards by buying behavior.
- It contrasts visionary enthusiasm with pragmatist caution to show why references from one group do not automatically persuade the other.

- Build mainstream proof before scaling mainstream sales expectations.
- Treat the chasm as a strategy change point.
